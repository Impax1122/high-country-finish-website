How to Hire a Commercial Sign Installer in Denver

Published September 17, 2026 · High Country Finish and Repair CO · Commercial Sign Installation

A sign is only as good as the install. The panel can be perfect and still end up crooked, anchored into nothing, or mounted somewhere the landlord never approved. Whether you are a business owner with a sign in a crate, a sign shop without an install crew, or a property manager rolling out signage across a portfolio, here is what to check before you hire an installer in Denver.

Who Hires a Sign Installer

  • Business owners who ordered a sign online or from an out-of-state shop and need it mounted.
  • Sign shops and print shops that fabricate but do not install, or whose crew is booked. This is install-only subcontracting, and it is a large part of our work.
  • General contractors and property managers who need signage placed at the end of a tenant improvement or across multiple buildings.
  • National brands and franchisors rolling out a program to Denver locations, with a sign package shipped from a central vendor.

What to Verify Before You Book

  • Insurance. Ask for a certificate of insurance showing general liability, and ask that you or the building be named as additional insured on the certificate. We carry $1 million in general liability coverage on every job. Anyone who hesitates on this question is not a commercial installer.
  • Training and certification. For vinyl work, 3M certification is the credential that means the installer has been tested on real material under real conditions. Our lead installer is a 3M Certified Graphics Installer with 25 years of sign installation experience.
  • Portfolio of similar work. Lobby signs, exterior panels, wall wraps, and vehicle graphics are different skills. Ask to see the type of install you need, in a building like yours.
  • References. A sign shop that subcontracts to the installer is the best reference there is, because they see the work on every job.

The Questions to Ask

  1. Is a site survey included? The survey is where the installer confirms the substrate, the mounting method, the exact location, power for illuminated signs, and access. A quote given without seeing the wall is a guess.
  2. Who handles the permit? Most exterior signs in Denver require a sign permit through Community Planning and Development, and illuminated signs need an electrical permit and a licensed electrician for the final connection. Interior signs generally do not need a permit. Know who is pulling it and whether it is in the price.
  3. Who approves the location? Landlords and property managers usually have sign criteria for the building: size limits, allowed locations, and mounting rules. Get the approval in writing before install day.
  4. What is the mounting plan? Brick, block, EIFS, metal panel, glass, and drywall each need different anchors and different judgment. The installer should be able to tell you exactly how the sign will attach and what the wall will look like if it is ever removed.
  5. Who supplies the lift? Anything above ladder height needs a lift or scaffold, and someone has to rent it, deliver it, and be qualified to run it. Sidewalk work downtown may also need a right-of-way permit.
  6. Can you install after hours? Retail, restaurants, and medical offices usually need evening or weekend installs. Confirm availability and any premium.
  7. What happens if the sign arrives damaged or wrong? A good installer inspects the sign before the crew rolls, not on the wall, and tells you the same day.
  8. Will I get photos? Completion photos with a level in frame are standard for commercial work, and they are what the sign shop or the brand needs to close out the job.

How Install-Only Pricing Works

Installers price one of three ways: a flat price per job after a survey, an hourly or daily crew rate plus equipment, or a per-unit rate for programs with many identical installs. Flat pricing is the norm for one-off commercial jobs. Hourly rates suit sign shops that send steady work and know how long their product takes to hang. Expect a minimum charge, a travel component outside the metro area, and lift rental billed at cost when one is required. We quote within 24 hours of a survey and serve roughly a one-hour radius of Denver.

How Sign Shop Subcontracting Works

If you fabricate signs and need an install partner, this is the arrangement most shops use with us:

  • You send the sign, the mounting hardware, the approved drawing, and any pattern or template. If you want us to produce the pattern from the survey, say so.
  • We survey, confirm the mounting approach with you, schedule with the site contact, and install.
  • You get completion photos and a short report the same day, addressed to you, for your client file.
  • We work under your name at your client's site. We do not market to your customers, and we do not hand out cards on your job. That is the whole basis of the relationship.

Shops use an install partner to take on work outside their crew's range, to cover a busy season, or to handle Denver installs for a sign shipped from another state. It also lets a wholesale shop offer an installed price without carrying a crew.

Red Flags

  • No certificate of insurance, or one that names a different company.
  • A price with no survey and no questions about the wall.
  • "We can put it anywhere" with no mention of the landlord or the permit.
  • No answer on who runs the lift.
  • Cash only, no invoice, no photos.
  • A vehicle wrap installer quoting a 200-pound exterior cabinet, or the reverse. The skills overlap less than people think.

How to Send a Good Install Request

The faster we can quote, the faster the sign goes up. Include:

  • The address and the exact location on the building or in the space, with photos.
  • The sign specs: size, weight, material, and the mounting method it was built for.
  • Drawings or a rendering showing the intended placement.
  • Any landlord sign criteria or approvals you already have.
  • Your timeline, and whether the install has to happen after hours.
  • Access notes: loading dock, parking, security check-in, ceiling height, and whether a lift can get in.

Install-only work is a specialty, and a good installer makes a sign shop or a property manager look good without ever being seen. If you have a sign that needs to go up in Denver or along the Front Range, send us the details and we will tell you how we would handle it.
